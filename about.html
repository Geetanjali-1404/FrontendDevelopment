GoBeyond Travels was founded to disrupt commercial over-tourism. We connect travelers directly with hereditary historians, tribal metalsmiths, and island monks to foster authentic cultural respect.

Our Genesis

The Story of GoBeyond Travels

In 2021, our founding team stood on the crowded southern bank of Hampi, surrounded by megaphone tours and commercial selfie sticks. Across the rushing Tungabhadra river, in ancient Anegundi, an elder was carving stone in total silence, reciting 14th-century Vijayanagara ballads to an empty courtyard.

India’s heritage is not trapped in glass vitrines or commercial monument parking lots. It lives in the 4,000-year-old lost-wax bell metal forges of Dandakaranya, the mud monasteries of high Spiti, the living aerial root bridges woven by Khasi mothers, and the crumbling havelis of Shekhawati.

We built GOBEYOND TRAVELS to bridge conscious global travelers with native guardians—ensuring that economic wealth reaches artisan households while monuments breathe freely away from over-crowding.

Supporting Local Economies

Our Community Economic Impact Statement

In typical commercial tourism, less than 15% of traveler spending trickles into host communities. At GoBeyond, we flipped the economic model on its head.

The 5 Pillars of Our Heritage Preservation Charter

01. Decolonized History

Tours are led strictly by hereditary residents who share indigenous perspectives rather than colonial or sanitized tourist board narratives.

02. Anti-Crowding Index

Our live Crowd Meter regulates traveler volume so ancient sanctuaries and monastic chapels never suffer structural footfall wear.

03. Fair Artisan Honorarium

When visiting artisan workshops, GoBeyond pays a respectful teaching fee for the master’s time, relieving pressure to push high-pressure shopping.

04. Sacred Grove Respect

Explicit protocols regarding photography of village shrines (Mata Gudi), sacred trees, and monastic sanctums are strictly maintained.

05. Youth Apprenticeships

A 5% Community Heritage Fund supports apprenticeships in endangered crafts like Majuli bamboo mask making and Athangudi glass-stenciled tiles.
